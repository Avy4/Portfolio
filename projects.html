SLORL

SLORL, an anagram of ROLLS. Roll through twenty progressively more difficult levels and get to the portals at the end. For an extra challenge, grab all the coins in each level. With your trusty hat along for the ride, anything is possible!

Key Mechanics

Design breakdownKey Decisions, Challenges, Outcomes

Key decisions

    Goal:
  • My plan for this game was to release it on web platforms like CoolMathGames and Armor Games.
    • Art style:
  • Chose a simple 2D pixel art reminicent of old flash games. It would also fit in with other games on these platforms.
  • Based all sprites off of three pastel color palettes, green → cream, pink → orange, and blue → cream.
  • Enabled for easy sprite creation and simple animations.
    • Level Design:
  • Every level is built off the same scene base.
  • Makes it easy to create and iterate on different level flows and puzzles.
  • The formula was to slowly introduce mechanics and combine these mechanics into progressively more difficult levels.

Challenges

    Level Design Difficulties: Teaching Mechanics
  • To cater to the intended playerbase (usually a younger demographic) I wanted to make game mechanics easy to learn and understand.
  • To do this I ended up putting visuals in the background of certain "tutorial" levels. This is something I would change!
  • Shoehorning visuals into the background of the levels is the least elegant method of teaching mechanics to the user.
  • If I were to make another puzzle platformer, I think I would make levels specifically to teach mechanics.
  • The distinction would be that rather than rather than building around a mechanic that is taught with visuals, I would build a level that guides the player towards discovering the mechanic themselves.
    • Compelling Levels
  • It's really very hard to make stages that are difficult, fun, but also mechanic dense.
  • The formula was to slowly introduce mechanics and combine these mechanics into progressively more difficult levels. This is something I would change!
  • With my current formula, I essentially made three or four levels in a row that depended on a specific mechanic. This led to some levels feeling one dimensional.
  • While this worked for a short game like SLORL, in the future I would lean towards using mechanics as a toolbox for level building rather than basing whole levels off a single mechanic.
    • UI/UX:
  • I really don't like how the home screen turned out, specifically in the Armor Games version.
  • The extra text and images required for branding make the homescreen look cluttered and ugly.
  • Not too big of a deal since this is only in one version but moving forward I would definitely make a seperate screen for credits.

Outcomes

  • An non-exclusive license was sold to Armor Games where it sits with ~10k plays and a 71% rating.
  • SLORL is also being hosted on Y8 games, and Kongregate where it gets less traffic.
  • While it didn't get accepted onto CoolMathGames, I think SLORL was a great learning opportunity. All things considered I would consider it a successful project!

Bounty

Set in a magical forest, you play as a bounty hunter fighting against a horde of dangerous bugs. Finished playing the included map? Try making your own beatmaps, all you need is an .mp3 file and quick fingers!

Role
Lead Developer
Team Size
Four
Engine / Tools
Unity, C#
Time
8 weeks
  • Mechanic Design
  • Scene Design
  • UI/UX
  • Custom Beatmap System (I/O)
  • Project Management

Key Mechanics

Design breakdownTeam Breakdown, Highlighted Personal Contributions, Challenges, Outcomes

Team Breakdown

  • My roles on the team were that of the project manager and the lead developer.
  • We decided to play to our strengths when deciding our roles, but this essentially meant we had three artists.
  • They took care of the sprite work, the UI/UX images, and sound design.
  • My role was to decide on how mechanics would be implemented, implement the mechanics, and do scene setup.

Highlighted Personal Contributions

    Beatmap Creation Menu
  • This is my favorite feature but it doesn't really have much to do with the mechanics of gameplay itself.
  • That being said, in my opinion it is an extremely important part of any rhythm game.
  • This feature allows for players to upload an .mp3 file and pop out beatmap .json that can be shared and played by anyone.
    • Title Screen
  • The title screen UI was entirely a team effort, but I wanted to highlight it here since it is in my opinion the best scene in the entire project.
  • While the sprite work was done by my very talented teammates, I gave important insight into what actually worked for a title screen.
  • Especially as it related to the more nitty gritty (i.e sprite dimensions, font choice, overall clarity)

Challenges

    Level Setup & Sprites
  • One big issue we had were sprites, more specifically their dimensions.
  • At first this didnt really seem that big of a deal to us since we could scale images, but we soon realised this is not the case.
  • One specific issue came with our backgrounds, since we had different people working on different parts of the background their dimensions weren't the same.
  • Even if you tried to scale them, they didn't look consistently the same. This is something I would change!
  • When doing a project by myself, I know what i'm working with as it relates to sprites so consistency isn't an issue.
  • The next time I work with a team though, something I would make sure of is that everyone is the on the same page when it relates to sprite consistency.
  • Specifically, on sprite dimensions, but also on color scheme, and overall theme.
    • Beatmap System
  • I recently had a teacher say that the first idea you think of is usually a bad idea.In the case of how I designed the Beatmap system, I would say this is 100% true.
  • My first instinct was to use a scriptable object for each beat.The idea behind this was that 1. You can see them in the inspector (which made it easier for my teammates to see what was going on) and 2. They are simple data containers. This is something I changed!
  • This idea, though it did work was uneccesarily complicated.
  • A simple class would do the same job, the only drawback being you couldn't see them in the inspector.
  • I did some research and figured out I could just make my new data class serializable by adding the [System.Serializable] tag.
  • After making this change, the entire process was much more straightforward while still being easily readable for my teammates.
  • It also really drove home the point that when making a new mechanic I should think on it a litte bit instead of diving straight into development.

Outcomes

  • This project was made for IMDM101 and it was well recieved by our professor.
  • The beatmap creation menu was an idea that he pitched, and I was kind of shocked I didn't think of it.
  • Working on this project was a fruitful experience, not only because I learned about more C# and Unity features but because it gave me a chance to work on a larger project alongside a team
  • Overall, I would say Bounty was an extremely successful project!
