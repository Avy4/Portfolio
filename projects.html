SLORL

SLORL, an anagram of ROLLS. Roll through twenty progressively more difficult levels and get to the portals at the end. For an extra challenge, grab all the coins in each level. With your trusty hat along for the ride, anything is possible!

Design breakdownDecisions, challenges, outcome

Key decisions

  • Art style — I decided to go with a simple cutesy pixel art design since it made it easy to make all the artwork cohesive. The color palette was one that I had found online.
  • Mechanics — The swap mechanic was something I had in mind for a while, it just so happened that when combined with the rotation mechanic it turned out quite fun. My favorite usage of the hat and rotation though are the interesting ways they interact with player movement. You can use the hat as a ramp to move in ways your player couldn't normally move. This made for some interesting tech that some of the levels were based off of.

Challenges

The most difficult part of developing SLORL was definitely level design. Making twenty increasingly difficult and compelling levels took longer than I would like to admit.

Outcome

Sold a non-exclusive license to Armor Games, and got SLORL uploaded to other websites like Kongregate and Y8 Games. Game did well for itself, definitely has some flaws but it currently sits around 10k plays and a 71% rating.

Bounty

Set in a magical forest, you play as a bounty hunter fighting against a horde of dangerous bugs. Like many other popular rhythm games, you click to the beat in a four lane system.

Design breakdownDecisions, challenges, outcome

My contributions

My team was made up of artists so I ended up developing the vast majority of the actual game. After we figured out our mechanics and systems I put it into C# code. I had full control over how I did this which led to some badly made systems, but I iterated and figured out a system we were all happy with. This was important since I needed my teamates who weren't accustomed to programming to understand as well. I also created the actual Unity scenes and put the entire game together.

As a fun side mission over the summer, I decided to make a custom beatmap creator in the game itself. Working with I/O isn't my forte (especially with how Unity handles music files) but I got it to work. The system itself could use some work and more user options and control over beat timing but I am happy with the current state given the type of project this was.
